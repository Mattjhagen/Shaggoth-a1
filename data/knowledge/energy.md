Energy is the capacity to do work or cause change, and it comes in many forms that can be converted between one another. The major forms of energy include kinetic, potential, thermal, electromagnetic, nuclear, electrical, chemical, and sound energy — each with distinct properties but interconvertible through physical and chemical processes. Kinetic energy is the energy of motion — a moving car, flowing water, or a thrown ball all possess kinetic energy proportional to their mass and the square of their velocity. Potential energy is stored energy based on position or configuration: gravitational potential energy in a raised object, elastic potential energy in a compressed spring, and chemical potential energy in molecular bonds. Thermal energy (heat) is the kinetic energy of atoms and molecules vibrating within a substance. Electromagnetic energy includes light, radio waves, X-rays, and gamma rays — forms of energy carried by photons across the electromagnetic spectrum. Nuclear energy is released when atomic nuclei split (fission) or combine (fusion); nuclear power plants use fission while the sun generates energy through fusion. Electrical energy is the movement of electric charges through a conductor, powering everything from light bulbs to computers. Sound energy is mechanical vibration transmitted through a medium like air or water. The law of conservation of energy states that energy cannot be created or destroyed, only converted from one form to another. All energy on Earth ultimately traces back to the sun (via solar radiation) or to primordial nuclear and gravitational energy from Earth's formation.
