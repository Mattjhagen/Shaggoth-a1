Aeroponics is a soilless plant cultivation method in which roots are suspended in air and periodically misted with a fine nutrient solution. Unlike hydroponics, where roots are submerged in water, aeroponic roots have constant access to oxygen in the air between misting cycles. This high oxygenation accelerates plant growth rates, often producing yields 30–40% faster than hydroponics and far faster than soil agriculture. Aeroponics uses significantly less water than both soil farming and hydroponics because the nutrient mist is recycled with minimal evaporation. The primary drawback is mechanical complexity: if the misting system fails, roots can dry out within hours and plants die rapidly. Aeroponics excels at producing high-value crops like herbs, leafy greens, and strawberries in controlled indoor environments. NASA has researched aeroponics for growing food in space due to its minimal water and weight requirements. High-pressure aeroponics uses very fine mist droplets (under 50 microns) to maximize root oxygenation and nutrient uptake. The technology requires precise environmental control of temperature, humidity, and nutrient concentration. Commercial aeroponic systems like AeroFarms have demonstrated it at scale for urban farming operations.
