DNA (deoxyribonucleic acid) is the molecule that carries genetic information in living organisms. DNA is found in the nucleus of cells and encodes the genetic blueprint for building and operating an organism. The genetic code stored in DNA determines inherited traits passed from parents to offspring. DNA consists of two strands twisted into a double helix, made of nucleotide bases: adenine, thymine, guanine, and cytosine. Genes are segments of DNA that encode instructions for making proteins. The genetic information in DNA is transcribed into RNA and then translated into proteins that carry out cellular functions. Mutations are changes in the genetic sequence that can affect traits or lead to disease. DNA replication copies the genetic material so it can be passed on during cell division.
